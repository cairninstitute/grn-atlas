Gene regulation research is rarely a single lookup. A useful workflow may need to move from a gene list to upstream regulators, evidence for an edge, a cross-species comparison, an RNAi or CRISPR option, and a defensible next experiment.

Darwin makes those operations explicit. Its 99 callable research skills cover network and regulon analysis, enrichment, expression and cell-state analysis, promoter and chromatin evidence, RNAi and CRISPR planning, cross-species transfer, and decision-ready validation planning.

We tested both individual tool use and multi-step workflow completion:

- GPT-5.6 Terra: 386/386 single-skill cases and 111/111 orchestration workflows
- Opus 4.6: 107/111 orchestration workflows
- Nemotron-3 Ultra: 95/111 orchestration workflows

The orchestration suite tests real chains: import-to-analysis, promoter and motif follow-up, intervention design and consequence prediction, cross-species transfer, phenotype-first prioritization, and evidence-to-validation handoffs.

These figures measure structured tool use and workflow completion, not independent proof of biological predictions. The goal is a more inspectable way to turn a research question into an atlas-grounded hypothesis and next step.
