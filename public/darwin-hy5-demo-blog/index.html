The Question

“Tell me everything about HY5 — who regulates it, what it controls, how that changes between cell types, and where the evidence comes from.”

That’s a single question to Darwin. What follows is what it found — all from one query against its atlas of 142,000 genes, 4.2 million interactions, and 7 million evidence records across 8 species.

HY5 (ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 5, AT5G11260) is a bZIP transcription factor and the master integrator of light signaling in plants. It sits at the crossroads of photomorphogenesis, hormone signaling, nutrient uptake, and flavonoid biosynthesis. It’s one of the most-studied TFs in plant biology — and Darwin’s atlas reflects that depth.

HY5’s Regulatory Neighborhood

HY5 doesn’t just regulate genes — it anchors an interconnected web. Its 2,606 targets span light response (the photosynthetic apparatus), nutrient signaling (nitrate and phosphate uptake), hormone pathways (auxin via IAA7/IAA14), and secondary metabolism (flavonoid biosynthesis via TT4 and MYB12). Of the 22 signed edges from perturbation and curation sources, 19 are activations and 3 are repressions — HY5 is predominantly an activator. The remaining edges rest on binding evidence (ChIP-seq, DAP-seq, motif prediction) which establishes physical occupancy but not regulatory direction. Of its 455 upstream regulators, 427 also appear as downstream targets, making them reciprocal binding partners (dashed). Whether reciprocal binding reflects true mutual regulation or co-occupancy between abundant TFs is an open question that requires signed evidence to resolve.

Cell-Type Resolution

Bulk databases would tell you HY5 regulates IAA7. Darwin tells you it regulates IAA7 in all four root cell types but PHR1 only in the stele and TT4 only in the epidermis. This is the difference between knowing a gene’s targets and understanding where those targets actually matter. The 6 ubiquitous targets (above the dashed line) are core light-response genes; the 13 tissue-specific targets reveal how HY5 wires different functions into different cell identities.

Evidence Audit

Darwin doesn’t just say “HY5 regulates CAB1” — it says why it thinks so, tracing each claim to its source. ConnecTF dominates with 8,117 perturbation experiment records (knockout/overexpression with measured downstream effects). Five independent ChIP-seq and binding databases add 2,990 physical binding records. Two literature-curated databases contribute 447 manually reviewed interactions. When Darwin reports a high-confidence edge, it’s because multiple independent lines of evidence — perturbation, binding, and curation — all agree.

The pattern is striking: these 20 selected partners collectively regulate over 200,000 targets. This suggests a densely interconnected control architecture where light signaling, hormone response (ABA, auxin), stress tolerance, and flowering time are all wired together through mutual binding. Whether these reciprocal binding relationships reflect true regulatory feedback — where perturbing one partner changes the other’s expression — remains to be tested with perturbation experiments for most pairs.

Cross-Species: From Arabidopsis to Crop

HY5 isn’t just an Arabidopsis gene. Darwin finds the tomato ortholog (Solyc08g061130.2) already in the atlas with 874 regulatory targets and 394 evidence records from PlantTFDB binding predictions. The cross-species view reveals what’s conserved and what’s diverged.

This is where Darwin’s cross-kingdom design pays off. The 2,606 HY5 targets in Arabidopsis — supported by perturbation, ChIP-seq, and binding-prediction evidence — become hypotheses for the tomato ortholog. Darwin’s orthology transfer pipeline identifies conserved targets across species, scores them by evidence strength, and flags them as inferred (never as measured). A tomato breeder asking “which genes does HY5 regulate in my crop?” gets both the direct tomato evidence and the much richer Arabidopsis knowledge, clearly labeled by source. Note that the 394 tomato evidence records come from PlantTFDB binding predictions, not perturbation experiments.

Evidence boundary: Every edge in every species carries its full provenance. Darwin never claims a binding prediction is a perturbation result, and never promotes an orthology transfer into a measured interaction. The honesty is the point: a researcher can trust the confidence scores because they know exactly what’s behind them.

This entire analysis — the network map, the cell-type resolution, the evidence audit, the feedback architecture, and the cross-species comparison — came from a single question to Darwin. No manual database queries, no spreadsheet joins, no literature mining. Just: “Tell me everything about HY5.”
